New Health Certificates Available Now
Starting October 2026, new health certificates are required for the export of non-commercial dogs, cats, ferrets, and birds and commercial dogs, cats, and ferrets to the European Union (EU). This is due to changes in EU legislation issued earlier this year (EU2026/131 and EU 2026/848).
The new non-commercial health certificates are effective October 1, 2026. Old certificates already in process can be endorsed on or before September 30, 2026.
The new commercial health certificate is effective October 17, 2026. Old certificates already in process can be endorsed on or before October 16, 2026.
Travel Requirements Based on Pet Type
Carefully read ALL of the requirements related to your pet on this page.
This page provides the most recent entry requirements and can change without notice.
It is the responsibility of the veterinarian to make sure the pet has met all health requirements of the destination country before issuing a health certificate.
Failure to meet the requirements may result in problems gaining certificate endorsement or difficulties upon arrival in the destination country.
Health certificates must be legible, accurate, and complete.
If only transiting the EU on the way to another country, non-commercial dogs, cats, ferrets, and birds may not need an EU health certificate (per EU 2026/131 Article 5). Learn more.
Transiting the European Union
Under EU Regulation 2026/131 Article 5, non-commercial dogs, cats, ferrets, or birds transiting the EU on their way to another country may not need an EU health certificate.
Only pets that meet the criteria for non-commercial movement qualify for this exemption. If the pet is traveling unaccompanied, they must have a commercial health certificate and are not eligible for the transit exemption.
If the pet will stay in an airport pet hotel between flights, be aware that the pet hotel may not be part of the international transit zone. Each port may define the transit zone differently. Confirm this with the port officials in advance and plan accordingly.

Microchip Requirements
Your pet must have a working microchip (also called a “transponder”) before getting its rabies vaccination for travel to the EU. Your veterinarian must always scan the microchip before giving your pet a rabies vaccination.
If your pet doesn’t have a microchip or the microchip doesn’t work, ask your veterinarian to implant an ISO‑compliant* microchip.
If your pet has a working microchip that is not ISO‑compliant, you must either travel with a scanner that can read it or have your veterinarian implant a second, ISO‑compliant microchip. Make sure both microchip numbers are listed on the health certificate.
*ISO‑compliant microchips are usually 15 digits long and meet specific international standards (ISO 11784 and 11785). You can check ISO compliance with the microchip manufacturer.
Rabies Vaccination Requirements
Pets that do not meet the EU's rabies requirements (EU 576/2013 Annex III) may be refused entry or quarantined upon arrival at the owner’s expense.
Rabies vaccines must be given by an authorized veterinarian.
After primary vaccination, your pet must wait at least 21 days—or the vaccine manufacturer's recommended immunity period—before traveling. Some manufacturers recommend waiting 30 days for the pet to gain immunity. Ask your veterinarian to confirm and record the correct period on the rabies certificate; this determines how long the vaccine is valid for travel.
A primary rabies vaccination is the first rabies shot your pet receives after microchipping or after any lapse in coverage. For all pets vaccinated in the United States:
A primary rabies vaccination is only valid for 1 year—even if your pet is an adult and received a 3-year vaccine.
A booster is required within 12 months. Any lapse, even one day, resets the vaccine to “primary” again (valid for only 1 year).
A booster is any rabies vaccination given within 1 year of the primary vaccination.
Valid for 1–3 years, depending on the manufacturer.
Pets may travel immediately after a booster (no 21‑day wait), as long as the primary vaccination is also listed on the health certificate.
Completing the Rabies Vaccination Table on the Health Certificate
Primary vaccination: The “From” date under "Validity of vaccination" must be 21 days (or the recommended immunity period) after the vaccination date.
Example (21 days):
Date of vaccination: 01/03/2019
Validity of vaccination: From: 22/03/2019 To: 01/03/2020
Example (30 days):
Date of vaccination: 01/03/2019
Validity of vaccination: From: 31/03/2019 To: 01/03/2020
Booster: The “From” date is the date the booster was given. No waiting period is required as long as there was no lapse between the primary vaccination and the booster.
Example:
Date of vaccination: 01/03/2019
Validity of vaccination: From: 01/03/2019 To: 01/03/2022
Important Reminders and Tips
If your pet’s most recent rabies vaccination before traveling to the EU is a booster, you must provide all relevant rabies certificates to show there was no lapse in coverage since the primary vaccination.
Rabies vaccinations do not count per EU rules if your pet got the vaccination:
Before it had a microchip, or
Without the microchip being scanned first.
Your pet should travel with all relevant rabies vaccination certificates. We strongly recommend making sure the microchip number appears on every rabies certificate.
Want to simplify your paperwork?
Ask your veterinarian to give your pet a 1‑year rabies vaccination (after scanning the microchip) at least 21 days but less than 1 year before your trip to the EU. Getting a fresh 1‑year vaccine—ideally 3–6 months before travel—means you only need one vaccination certificate, no matter your pet’s past rabies vaccination history.
This also makes it easier for the USDA endorsement office to review, so you can get your endorsed certificate back faster.
If your pet is less than 16 weeks old and cannot meet the above requirements, follow this guidance.
The EU has two types of pet health certificates: non-commercial and commercial.
Both require an accredited veterinarian to issue (complete, sign, and date) the certificate and USDA to endorse it (countersign and emboss/stamp) before your pet travels. Read the information below carefully to learn which certificate you will need to use given your logistical circumstances.
Non-Commercial Health Certificate
When To Use It
The pet owner is traveling with the pet on the same flight, or a designated person** is traveling with the pet no later than 5 days after the pet owner, and five or fewer pets are traveling on the same movement.
You may also use the non-commercial certificate for six or more pets if they are traveling for specific events like competitions, exhibitions, or sporting events and the pet owner or designated person is traveling with the pets.
What You Need To Know
The non-commercial health certificate is valid for 30 days after the accredited veterinarian issues it.
USDA must endorse the completed certificate within 10 days of your pet’s arrival in the EU. See "Step 3" below for more information on endorsement.
The final page includes a Declaration that must be completed and signed by the owner and/or designated person** as applicable. This Declaration must travel with the pet and the certificate.
You may use the non-commercial certificate to travel within the EU for up to 6 months from the day of entry into the EU, as long as your pet’s rabies vaccination remains valid.
Dogs entering Finland, Malta, Ireland (including Northern Ireland), or Norway after entering the EU must receive a tapeworm treatment from an EU veterinarian 1–5 days before entry into those countries.
The certificate must arrive with the pet to the port of entry.
View the Certificate
A USDA-accredited veterinarian must issue the certificate and submit it through the Veterinary Export Health Certification System (VEHCS) for endorsement by USDA. A printed copy of the completed, endorsed certificate must accompany the pet(s).

Commercial Health Certificate
When To Use It
The pet owner or a designated person** cannot travel with the pet.
You are traveling with 6 or more pets.
The pet is changing owners when it arrives in the EU.
What You Need To Know
Your pet(s) must be inspected by a USDA-accredited veterinarian within 48 hours of departing the United States. Discuss your travel logistics with your veterinarian and plan accordingly.
The certificate must arrive with the pet to the port of entry.
View the Certificate
The USDA-accredited veterinarian must issue the certificate and submit it through the Veterinary Export Health Certification System (VEHCS) for endorsement by USDA. A printed copy of the completed, endorsed certificate must accompany the pet(s).

After your pet's accredited veterinarian issues the health certificate, have them submit it to USDA for endorsement through the Veterinary Export Health Certification System (VEHCS).
Non-commercial health certificate: The endorsement must happen within 10 days of your pet's arrival in the EU.
Commercial health certificate: The endorsement must happen within 48 hours after the accredited veterinarian issues the certificate.

International Health Certificate (92.88 KB)
Before you travel, carefully read all requirements for taking pet birds to the European Union (EU).
The information below applies to pet birds traveling to the EU in a group of five or fewer.
Identification of Pet Birds
Pet birds must have an identification number, such as a clip, leg band, microchip (transponder), or tag.
The only exception is if the birds will be moved to an approved quarantine facility in the EU. In that case, a physical description of the birds is acceptable. However, APHIS must seal the birds in their container, and the seal number must be recorded on the health certificate.
Health Certificate Requirements
There are two options for preparing pet birds for export to the EU. Select the option that applies to your situation to obtain the correct health certificate. For both options:
A USDA-accredited veterinarian must examine the pet birds and issue an EU health certificate within 48 hours of travel or on the last working day before departure to the EU.
After the veterinarian issues the certificate, it must be endorsed by APHIS.
Important: If the pet birds will return to the United States, certain re-entry requirements may need to be completed before leaving the United States. View the requirements for pet birds returning to the United States.

Health Certificate for Pet Birds: Option 1 (357.68 KB)
Use the health certificate below if the pet bird was isolated from other birds for at least 14 days before leaving the United States and tested for avian influenza H5 and H7 antigens or genomes with a virus isolation or a real‑time polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) test.
Regardless of test type:
Both a cloacal swab and an oropharyngeal swab must be collected by a USDA-accredited veterinarian no earlier than 7 days after the 14‑day isolation period begins. The day isolation starts is considered day 0.
Testing must be performed at a laboratory approved by APHIS to conduct avian influenza testing. To learn more, visit the National Veterinary Services Laboratories (NVSL) or the National Animal Health Laboratory Network (NAHLN).
